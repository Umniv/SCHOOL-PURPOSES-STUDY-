Core concept

Physical activity, fitness & recreation

The foundational definitions before breaking fitness into its components.

Physical activity

Regular, moderate bodily movement — roughly 30 minutes to 1 hour, 3 to 4 times a week or more — that raises your heart rate. Rest or easy days in between let your body recover from exertion.

Physical fitness

The ability to carry out everyday tasks without becoming short of breath, tired, or sore — a reflection of how well your heart, lungs, muscles, and joints perform.

Recreational activities

Activities pursued during leisure time, done purely to refresh and enjoy oneself.

Components of physical fitness

Fitness components fall into two families. Health-related components support everyday wellbeing; skill-related components support performance in sport.

Health-related Skill-related

Health-related components

MS

Muscular strength

The amount of force your muscles can exert — the basis for lifting, pushing, kicking, and throwing with power, and for supporting your bones and joints.

ME

Muscular endurance

The ability to use a group of muscles repeatedly over a long period without tiring easily — such as pedaling a bicycle up a hilly track without stopping.

FL

Flexibility

The ability to bend, twist, reach, and move your joints through their full range — used when striking a tennis ball or touching your toes while seated.

CE

Cardiorespiratory endurance

The ability to sustain activity that demands increased oxygen over time. Running, walking, biking, and swimming all build this component.

BC

Body composition

The proportion of fat tissue to lean tissue in the body.

Skill-related components

AG

Agility

The ability to rapidly and accurately change the direction of the whole body in space while keeping your balance.

BA

Balance

The ability to maintain equilibrium — control of your body — whether stationary or moving.

CO

Coordination

The ability to use your senses and body parts together to perform motor tasks smoothly and accurately.

PO

Power

The ability to perform a task quickly and forcefully. Expressed as strength × speed.

RT

Reaction time

The ability to respond quickly to what you hear, see, or feel.

SP

Speed

The ability to move quickly from one point to another.

Applied skill

Body Mass Index (BMI)

The standard health professionals use to determine whether a person's weight is healthful, based on body composition.

Formula
BMI = weight (kg) ÷ height² (m)

Conversion factors: 1 m = 100 cm · 1 inch = 2.54 cm · 1 ft = 12 inches

Worked example

Compute the BMI of Allan, who weighs 65 kg and stands 5 ft 7 in tall.

Height5 ft 7 in = 67 in × 2.54 = 170.18 cm = 1.70 m
Height²1.70 m × 1.70 m ≈ 2.90 m²
BMI65 ÷ 2.90 ≈ 22.4
Result: Normal weight

Why it matters

Benefits of active participation in sports & recreation

Regular participation pays off across four dimensions of life.

HB

Health benefits

A road map to good health and longevity: improved quality of life, lower blood pressure, reduced arthritis pain, and weight loss.

PB

Physical benefits

Improvement of skills specific to a particular activity, plus relaxation, rest, and revitalization.

MB

Mental & emotional benefits

Release of stress from everyday demands, greater personal confidence and self-esteem, and a sense of achievement.

SB

Social benefits

Bonding with family and friends, new friendships and acquaintances, and stronger social networks and community identity.

Whole-person model

The 7 dimensions of wellness

Fitness is one slice of a larger picture. These seven dimensions together define overall wellness.

1

Physical

The health of your physical body.

2

Emotional

Your psychological and emotional perspective about yourself and the world around you.

3

Intellectual

Open-mindedness and an intelligent response to stimuli, decision-making skill, and lifelong learning.

4

Social

Your interaction and relationships with others.

5

Career

Personal satisfaction from your career or job, and your contribution as a productive member of society.

6

Environmental

Your responsibility to take care of the environment.

7

Spiritual

Your religious beliefs and practices, and — more importantly — their effect on every other dimension of your wellbeing.

Planning framework

The FITT principle

Four variables to adjust when designing a personal exercise plan.

  1. F
    Frequency — how often you exercise. Aim for a balanced routine that challenges the body while leaving time to rest, adapt, and heal — often two to three, or three to five, days a week.
  2. I
    Intensity — how much effort or work you exert. Avoid overloading or over-straining your body; monitor your heart rate and resistance as you train.
  3. T
    Type — what kind of exercise fits your goal. Running, cycling, and swimming build the heart; lifting weights builds muscle.
  4. T
    Time — how long each session lasts. Duration depends on the intensity and type of exercise chosen.

PATHFIT 101 · Movement Competency Training, Week 4

Warm-up exercises

Preparation for the body before rigorous physical activity. Warming up raises your heart rate, increases blood flow, and warms your muscles — helping you avoid injury and move better through your workout.

1

Protects your muscles

Warm muscles stretch and bend easily instead of tearing.

2

Helps your heart

Raises your heart rate gradually, so it doesn't jump straight into heavy work.

3

Sends more oxygen

Moving blood carries vital oxygen directly to your working muscles.

4

Wakes up your brain

Sharpens your balance, focus, and reaction time.

5

Lubricates your joints

Fluid moves into the joints so they move smoothly, without pain.

The 11-move warm-up sequence

  1. 1Jogging on the spot — 15 seconds
  2. 2Star jumps — 10x
  3. 3Arm circles
  4. 4Arm scissors
  5. 5Jump lunges — 10x
  6. 6Calf raises
  7. 7Ankle rolls
  8. 8Step & swing — 10x
  9. 9Toe touches — 15x
  10. 10Open and close the gate — 15x
  11. 11Knee hops — 10x

Cool-down note

Repeat the same sequence for your cool-down after rigorous physical activity.
